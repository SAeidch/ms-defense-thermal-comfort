Three numbers frame the problem. First, fewer than 20 percent of buildings reach the target of 80 percent thermally satisfied occupants. Second, personalized conditioning has been shown in simulations to save up to about 60 percent of HVAC energy, so knowing how occupants feel pays off. Third, the standard model, PMV, needs six inputs, and mean radiant temperature, air speed, clothing and metabolic rate are hard to measure continuously. A reliable, cheap comfort signal is the missing input for occupant-centered control.

The final aim of this work was to see whether we can use fewer variables and still reach acceptable accuracy. Two constraints: fewer variables, going from eleven signals in the chamber to three sensed signals, and fewer labels, 596 local votes against 44,124 in the public database. The strategy is transfer learning: pretrain a Transformer on ASHRAE DB II, then plug in a small adapter for the new building and sensors.

Related work: two lines that meet in this thesis

Open question: recent work transfers comfort models across datasets and people, but can a Transformer pretrained on ASHRAE DB II be adapted, with a small adapter, to a 3-signal wearable and environmental setup, and does the backbone matter for that transfer?
Two lines of work meet here. The machine learning line, from NeurIPS and ICML: transferable features and domain adaptation, the Transformer, adapters for parameter-efficient transfer, and recent pretrained foundation models for time series and wearable data. The thermal comfort line: physiological models, recurrent networks, early Transformer and transfer-learning comfort models, and a cluster of 2026 work. Soleimanijavid and Konstantzos transfer features learned on ASHRAE DB II to a small dataset; Yang and colleagues adapt wearable comfort models across people; Liu and Gou show that accuracy drops sharply on unseen occupants. What remained open is whether a Transformer pretrained on DB II can be adapted through a small adapter to a three-signal setup, and whether the backbone matters for that transfer.

Two experiments, presented as two steps. Step one is the full-sensing chamber study: seven environmental signals, three wearable signals and a thermal camera. Step two is the main work: an occupied office and classroom, where the model uses only air temperature, humidity and smartwatch heart rate; CO2, CO and light were logged but deliberately left out. The campaign produced 596 synchronized observations. Each is a 12-step, five-minute sequence from a session, paired with a thermal sensation vote. Warm discomfort is rare, under five percent. Let me state the problem formally. The source domain is ASHRAE DB II: 44,124 labeled sequences, each a 12-by-17 matrix. The target domain is our field data: 596 labeled sequences, each 12 by 6, with the same three classes. The model is a composition: an input map h, the positional embedding P, six encoder layers E-phi, last-token pooling pi, and a linear classifier. Training has two stages. First, pretraining finds theta-star on the source. Then, for adaptation, the input map is replaced by the adapter a-omega, and we minimize the same weighted loss on the target over only the adapter, the sixth encoder layer and the classifier, keeping the positional embedding and layers one to five fixed. That is about 562 thousand of 3.2 million parameters, roughly 18 percent. For the BiLSTM, the adapter maps into layer two, which is retrained with the classifier.

ASHRAE records are snapshots: one set of conditions at the moment of the survey. Both backbones are sequence models, so we built a one-hour history for every record. The actual record becomes the last of 12 five-minute steps. For the 11 earlier steps we add zero-mean Gaussian jitter to the five transient variables, air temperature, humidity, air velocity, metabolic rate and SET, and keep the static features constant. Then we add first differences of the transient variables, which gives 17 features per step and a tensor of N by 12 by 17. I can regenerate the jitter to show that each history is one random sample. This history is synthesized, not measured, and I return to that in the limitations.

This is the T3C architecture. A linear layer projects the 17 features at each step into 256 dimensions; a learned positional embedding adds order. Then six encoder layers, each with eight-head self-attention and a feed-forward network that expands to 512, with residual connections and layer normalization. The small heat map shows what attention computes: for each of the 12 steps, a weight over all 12 steps; the values are illustrative. We pool the last token, the actual survey moment, which through attention already summarizes the earlier steps. A linear layer gives three class probabilities. Here is the encoder in equations, using the row-vector convention. Equation one: the 17 features are projected to 256 dimensions, the learned positional embedding is added, and LayerNorm is applied. Equations two and three: each of the eight heads forms queries, keys and values of 32 dimensions and a 12-by-12 attention matrix with the scaled softmax. Equation four concatenates the heads and projects them back with W-O. Equations five and six are the two post-norm sub-layers: attention, then a ReLU feed-forward network that widens to 512, each with dropout, a residual connection and LayerNorm. Equation seven is last-token pooling and the classifier. On the right: per layer, self-attention costs on the order of T-squared times d operations with constant sequential depth, and the path from step one to step twelve is a single hop; a recurrent layer needs T sequential steps and an 11-step path. With only 12 steps and a width of 256, the attention term is small. The parameter formula gives 527,104 per layer, and about 3.17 million for the whole model.

For the baseline, these are the standard LSTM equations: input, forget and output gates and a candidate g, a cell state that is a gated sum of the previous cell state and the candidate, and a hidden state. The bidirectional layer concatenates a forward and a backward pass into 512 dimensions, and the classifier reads the second layer at step twelve. On the right is the structural contrast. With attention, step twelve is a weighted sum over all twelve value vectors, one hop, with explicit weights that sum to one. If I unroll the cell-state recursion exactly, step k reaches step twelve through a product of twelve minus k forget gates, each between zero and one. This is a structural difference between the backbones; I do not claim it is the tested reason for the adaptation gap.

This is the core method. I start from the pretrained T3C. Then the input layer is swapped: the original 17-to-256 projection, learned on ASHRAE, is bypassed, and the adapter, two linear projections from 6 to 128 to 256, takes its place. Then the stack is frozen: the positional embedding and encoder layers one to five keep their ASHRAE weights, and only the adapter, layer six and the classifier are trained. The bar on the right shows the parameter budget: about 0.56 of 3.2 million parameters, roughly 18 percent, are trained. For the BiLSTM, layer one is tied to the 17 ASHRAE inputs, so its adapter enters at layer two, which is retrained from its pretrained weights. During adaptation we balanced classes only in the training partition and kept 120 real observations per run for validation, of which only six are warm.

Fewer variables and far less data, at a similar accuracy level

The two numbers come from different datasets with different class balance: a comparable level, not a head-to-head test. The 71% source figure also uses two survey inputs that sensors cannot provide, so it is an optimistic reference. This slide answers the research question. The source model saw 17 features per step, including two subjective survey items, and 44,124 labeled observations, and reached 71 percent on the ASHRAE hold-out. The adapted model sees six features, only three of them sensed, and used 596 observations in total, about 476 for training and 120 for validation per run, about 1.4 percent of the source size. It reached 73 percent on our local validation data, against a 50 percent majority-class baseline. These are different datasets, so this is not a head-to-head comparison, but it shows that a reduced sensor set plus transfer learning reaches a comparable level of performance.
04

Discussion

Why the backbones diverged, and what the evidence does not show
Let me discuss why the two backbones behaved differently, and the limits of the evidence.

Same source accuracy, different transferability

01 · CONNECTION POINT

Where the adapter plugs in

The T3C adapter replaces only the linear input projection, so all six pretrained attention layers still process the sequence (five frozen). The BiLSTM adapter replaces a whole recurrent layer, so one of its two temporal layers is lost and the other must be retrained.

02 · STABILITY

Lower variance across seeds

1.36 vs 2.39 pp SD in accuracy, and every seed favors the Transformer, consistent with a more stable transfer.

03 · SIGNAL

Heart rate may help in context

Three sensed signals reached 73% three-class accuracy, consistent with physiological comfort studies (Chen et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2021). No run without heart rate was made, so its contribution is not isolated.

These are interpretations, not controlled tests: architecture and trained capacity were not separated, and with six warm cases per run the per-class differences are fragile.
Why did the backbones diverge after transfer when they tied on the source? First, where the adapter plugs in: in T3C it replaces only the linear input projection, so all six pretrained attention layers still process the sequence, five of them frozen. In the BiLSTM it replaces a whole recurrent layer, so one of the two temporal layers is lost and the other must be retrained. Second, stability: the Transformer adapter varied less across seeds and won every seed. Third, the signal: heart rate plus the environment reached about 73 percent, although we did not run a model without heart rate, so its own contribution is not isolated. These are interpretations, not controlled experiments.

Limitations

Data

  • Rare warm discomfort: 6 warm cases per validation run, so per-class recall is fragile
  • Small cohort: 15 participants, two rooms, one climate, November–January only
  • New occupants: how the 120 validation observations were drawn is not documented, and they may share participants with training; leave-one-participant-out not evaluated
  • No separate local test set: if the 120 validation observations also selected the reported epoch (the mean curves peak below the reported means), the adapter scores are optimistic

Source model

  • Pseudo-temporal sequences: the ASHRAE history is synthesized, not measured
  • Subjective inputs: thermal acceptability and preference are source inputs that sensors cannot provide (the adapter does not use them)
  • Hold-out reuse: the ASHRAE hold-out also drove the learning-rate schedule

Comparison

  • Architecture and trained capacity are confounded: the BiLSTM adapter retrains its whole second layer, T3C only its top layer
  • No from-scratch or classical baseline on the same local split, so the gain from pretraining is not isolated
  • Three seeds per configuration

Step 1 chamber study

  • 5 participants; setpoints rose over the campaign, so IAQ variables are confounded with temperature
I want to be transparent about the limitations. On the data side: the warm class is very small, the cohort is small and covers one winter season, generalization to new occupants is untested, and there is no separate local test set, so if the validation data also selected the reported epoch the scores are optimistic. On the source side: the ASHRAE histories are synthesized, two source inputs are survey answers a sensor cannot provide, and the hold-out also drove the learning-rate schedule. The comparison does not separate architecture from trained capacity, and there is no baseline without pretraining on the same split. And the chamber study had five participants, with temperature confounded with session order.

To conclude. First, reduced sensing is feasible: three sensed signals plus date-derived context reached 73 percent accuracy on local three-class comfort. Second, transfer is feasible with little data: 596 local observations in total were enough to adapt the model, although a baseline without pretraining is still needed to isolate the gain from transfer. Third, the backbone may matter for transfer: the two backbones tied on the source data, but the Transformer adapter led in all three seeds after transfer. And fourth, the full-sensing chamber study found air temperature to be the dominant signal, consistent with the reduced design.

Future work

Stronger evaluation

  • Leave-one-participant-out
  • Baseline without pretraining
  • More warm-discomfort samples
  • Source models without survey inputs

Richer data

  • A full year of data
  • Wrist skin temperature
  • More rooms and climates

Toward control

  • Step-wise predictions for HVAC feedback
  • Physics priors (PMV/SET) + learned residual
  • LLM reasoning layer grounded in ASHRAE 55 (ongoing)
Future work has three directions. Stronger evaluation: leave-one-participant-out validation, a baseline without pretraining, more warm samples, and source models without survey inputs. Richer data: a full year so the seasonal encoding covers its whole cycle, wrist skin temperature, and more rooms and climates. Toward control: step-wise predictions for HVAC feedback, physics priors, and connecting predictions to actions, which we are exploring with an LLM reasoning layer grounded in ASHRAE Standard 55.
